# Setting-Up-MFA-for-AWS-Account-Security
Documentation and best practices for implementing Multi-Factor Authentication to secure your AWS root and IAM users

The first line of defense in securing an AWS account is defining a robust account-wide password policy. This process is managed directly from the AWS IAM Console by following these steps:

Navigate to Account Settings: On the left-hand navigation pane of the IAM dashboard, click on Account settings

<img width="1916" height="765" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0732e4ac-319f-4030-b515-6f01823d2e52" />

Modify the Policy: Locate the Password policy section and select the option to edit your configuration

You should then be prompted to choose between two policy types:

IAM Default: You can choose to enforce the standard, pre-configured security requirements provided out-of-the-box by AWS

<img width="1886" height="932" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2865c22a-f348-4631-9c36-4bc422b49852" />

Custom: Alternatively, you can build a tailored policy to enforce stricter compliance rules, including:

Complexity Requirements: Mandating a minimum character length and requiring a mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numerical digits, and non-alphanumeric (special) characters

Expiration Limits: Setting passwords to automatically expire after a specific timeframe (e.g., 90 days), and deciding whether expiration requires an administrative reset

User Permissions: Choosing whether to permit users to self-change their passwords

Prevention of Reuse: Restricting users from recycling old passwords

<img width="893" height="665" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b3043c0b-0547-4157-b8c8-0178be1f4cb5" />

Once password complexity requirements are applied, proceed to the next section to configure Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

On the same screen, click on your account name in the top right corner and select Security credentials

<img width="958" height="1028" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fe84e1ca-c0a5-4625-8c38-937760a12848" />

Once the page has loaded click on Assign MFA

<img width="953" height="624" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e0940da3-cb8d-49b5-a7ee-cced75d8b888" />

Next, enter a unique name to easily identify your MFA device

<img width="959" height="612" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1f961755-de93-4696-8abd-b3587f0928b6" />

Select your preferred authentication method from the available options: Passkey, Authenticator app, or a Hardware TOTP token. For the purposes of this walkthrough, we will proceed with the Authenticator app and click Next at the bottom of the page

<img width="857" height="655" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b0cb605a-7eaf-4881-b5e1-49dff8437067" />

We can now proceed with setting up the app. 

Step 1: Download a compatible virtual MFA application (such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, or Duo Mobile) onto your mobile device

<img width="713" height="143" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e9b2257c-cd1f-4a6c-a8ba-52bbb49b9943" />

Step 2: Open the application on your device. On the AWS Management Console web page, click Show QR code, then use your app to scan the displayed graphic. Alternatively, manually input the provided secret key into your application

<img width="487" height="292" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e3fffdba-0b35-469e-90b0-9a9c0e0d55fd" />

Step 3: Retrieve and enter two consecutive Time-based One-Time Password (TOTP) codes generated by your application to synchronize the device with AWS

<img width="498" height="260" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8e6579c2-d234-4991-8d3c-2485c62eb596" />

Click the Assign MFA button at the bottom of the interface to complete the binding process

A confirmation banner will appear at the top of the screen indicating that the virtual MFA device was successfully assigned

<img width="1916" height="487" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/7edc34b0-8ee5-4d10-9168-bc743d544832" />

To confirm MFA setup was successful, in the top-right corner of the AWS Management Console, click on your account name or user ID dropdown menu. Select Sign Out from the list to clear your current session. Return to your custom AWS IAM sign-in URL. Provide your IAM username and password, then click Sign In

Upon successful password validation, the console will redirect you to an MFA verification page. Open your registered Authenticator app on your mobile device. Locate the 6-digit Time-based One-Time Password (TOTP) tied to your AWS account, enter it into the console text field, and click Sign in

<img width="963" height="1025" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/98dcc540-47ad-471c-88cd-83bd87d7ac86" />

You should now be logged in to your AWS Management Console Home and have completed the addition of an extra level of security on your AWS account
